Reading Lead Performance 360

One screen covering where your business is coming from, how it is trending, what is still open, and whether you keep what you win. This is a map of which part answers which question.

Start here: where do I look?

Five areas, top to bottom. Each answers a different kind of question, so it is worth knowing which one to go to rather than reading the whole screen every time.

The five areas

Reading down the screen, each area narrows the question: from totals, to trend, to what is still in play, to whether it sticks.

Area 1 · Top of screen

YTD scorecard

Answers: where has the year got to so far?

Six tiles covering January through the current month. Each shows one headline figure, a smaller paired figure beneath it, and how it compares with the same period last year.

New Prospects
Fresh opportunities that are not existing customers. Paired with how many of your customers sit under the 5K revenue band.
Cross-Sell Prospects
Opportunities to sell more into accounts you already have. CS throughout the dashboard means cross-sell. Paired with customers above 5K.
New Customers
Accounts won for the first time, and the revenue they brought.
Cross-Sell Customers
Existing accounts that bought again, and the revenue from it.
Total Customers
New plus cross-sell, with total revenue beneath.
Conversion %
Share of prospects that became customers. The one tile with no paired figure.

Area 2 · Trend

Monthly and Quarterly performance

Answers: which direction is this going?

Three measures tracked over time, each in its own panel with its own scale:

Revenue
Closed revenue in the period.
Customers
How many accounts that revenue came from.
Leads
What entered the top of the funnel — the leading indicator for the other two.

Monthly runs January to the current month and gains a new month automatically as the year goes on. Quarterly shows all four quarters, so quarters that have not started yet are intentionally empty.

Area 3 · Still open

Pipeline and loss

Answers: what is still in play, and what did we drop?

Qualified Lost Customers
Genuine opportunities that were qualified and then lost — not unqualified noise.
Lost Revenue
What those losses were worth.
Pipeline Revenue quarter
Open pipeline with a close date in the current quarter. Your near-term picture.
Pipeline Revenue FY
Open pipeline across the whole financial year.
Pipeline Overdue
Open pipeline whose close date has already passed. This is the action list — deals that need re-dating, re-qualifying, or closing out.
Forecasted vs Attainment
How far through the forecast you are, as a bar with the attained and forecast figures beneath. The bar turns amber then red as the gap widens.

Area 4 · Retention

Churned vs booked, and monthly closures

Answers: does the business we win actually stay?

Churned vs booked customers puts what you signed against what you lost, month by month. Booked climbing while churn stays flat is healthy growth; both climbing together means you are replacing customers rather than adding them.

Monthly PSE's / closures tracks closure rates as percentages, so PSE's, revenue and customers sit comparably on one axis.

Area 5 · Channel

Guided selling vs self service

Answers: how much of this needed a person?

Two charts, one counting customers and one counting revenue, splitting each into guided selling (a rep was involved) and self service (the customer closed themselves). The colours stay consistent across both, so you can read the pair together.

Revenue per channel is usually the more revealing of the two: a large self-service share of customers but a small share of revenue tells you self service is winning the small accounts.

Controls along the top

Everything in the filter row applies to the whole screen at once, so the figures always agree with each other.

Year
Which year you are looking at, one at a time. The current year runs to this month; past years show in full. Unlike the others this is single-choice — the charts share one month axis, so two years cannot sit on it at once.
Scope
All records, My records (only yours), or My team (yours plus everyone reporting to you). This follows who is signed in, so the same dashboard shows each person their own numbers.
Region · BU · Service
Tick as many as you like — EMEA and APAC together, for instance. Ticking nothing means all of them. Each list has Select all and Deselect all, and a search box once there is enough to search. Different filters narrow each other, so two regions inside one service gives you exactly that.
BU Head · Manager · Rep
Narrow to people and everyone under them, again as many as you like. The three step down the reporting line: tick a BU head and the Manager list shrinks to their managers, tick a manager and the Rep list shrinks to their reps. A head or manager shows their whole team rolled up; a rep shows just that person.
vs Last year
Draws last year's actuals behind this year's, and adds a year-on-year percentage to each panel heading.
Target
Draws the quota line and shades any shortfall, so you can see when you fell behind rather than just by how much.
Trend
Fits a straight line through the visible range — useful when a bumpy series makes the direction hard to judge. It re-fits when you zoom.
Reset
Puts every filter, overlay and zoom back to the default view in one click. It stays greyed out while nothing is actually filtered, so it never looks live when there is nothing to clear. Your chosen chart shapes are left alone — those are a display preference, not a filter hiding data.
Refresh
The blue circular-arrow button. Re-fetches the current slice; the charts hold their existing picture, dimmed, while the new figures arrive.

Things you can do to a chart

Hover
Shows every series at that month, the change from the previous month, and — when the overlays are on — last year's figure and the gap to target.
Click a point
Opens the actual records behind it, so you can go from a number straight to the deals. Click a record to open it in CRM.
Drag across
Zooms into a range of months, and every month chart follows so the whole screen stays on the same window. A chip appears at the top to clear it.
Click a legend key
Hides that series so you can isolate the other one.
Bars · Lines · Area
Switches how the same data is drawn. Bars are easier for comparing individual months; lines make direction clearer.
Table
Shows the exact figures behind the chart. Every number is reachable here without hovering.

Six things people misread

Green does not always mean up. The colour follows whether the change is good, not which way the arrow points. On Churned Customers, Lost Revenue and Pipeline Overdue, a fall shows ▼ green because falling is what you want.

A gap is missing data, not zero. Where a month has no data the line breaks and a tile reads –. It is deliberately not drawn as zero, because zero is a real and very different result.

Revenue, Customers and Leads sit in separate panels on purpose. They are measured in different units, so putting them on one axis would let the scale decide which line looks bigger. Separate panels keep each one honest; the shared crosshair still lets you compare a month across all three.

Pipeline figures are a snapshot, not a running total. Pipeline, overdue and forecast show where things stand right now. They are not the sum of the year's months, so do not expect them to add up like revenue does.

The most specific person wins. If you pick a rep, the Manager, BU Head and Scope choices above are ignored rather than combined — you get that rep. Combining them could quietly produce an empty screen that looks like no business, so the narrowest choice simply takes over. Set the ones you are done with back to All to widen again.

Stacked and 100% answer different questions. Stacked shows real totals, so the column grows as business grows. 100% shows only the mix, so the column is always full height — useful for spotting a shift in channel share, useless for telling whether the business grew.
